Statement

I study how systems change — and visualize how it feels. Structure meets surrender; the image holds the moment between them.

I’m a computational biologist and visual artist. I spent years observing living systems — how they adapt, break, and reorganize. Then I met states with no scientific terminology — only sensation. I now apply the same method to interior experience, rendering emotion as direct visual observation. Some states don’t have language yet, but they can have form.

I work at the threshold between structure and surrender. I set constraints—composition, color systems, biological references — then introduce variables that destabilize them. What emerges is the interval between plan and accident, held open long enough to witness. Some works use biological imagery because certain feelings behave like cellular processes; others dissolve into abstraction because some states resist external form.

I paint what resists language but demands to be seen. The work asks you to look until pattern gives way to sensation—and to notice what it does to you right now.

Gallery

Explore the gallery in chronological order or organized by themes. Use filters on the left to refine your view.

Identity & Self

The collapse and reconstruction of who we think we are. Every portrait is a question the body asks itself.

Transformation & Evolution

Nothing stays still. Everything that survives must change, and every change leaves a scar that becomes structure.

Memory & Time

What remains when the moment is gone. Time doesn't heal—it reshapes, distorts, and occasionally gifts us with fragments we can almost hold.

Control & Entropy

The illusion of order versus the honesty of chaos. We build systems to feel safe, but entropy is the only truth that never lies.

Perception & Noise

What we see is never what's there. The signal fights the static, and sometimes the static wins—and that's when it gets interesting.

Alienation & Belonging

The distance between where you are and where you're supposed to be. We don't belong to places; we haunt them until they feel like home.

Survival & Collapse

The body as both fortress and prison. To survive is to carry the weight of every moment you didn't give up, even when collapsing felt like relief.

Death & Renewal

Every ending is a门 that refuses to close completely. What dies doesn't disappear—it becomes soil for what grows next.
